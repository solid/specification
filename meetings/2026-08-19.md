## 3. Key Discussion: Developer Survey Analysis

### 3.1 Survey Overview
- The group reviewed the results of a recent developer survey that received **18 complete responses**.
- **Key Finding:** Most respondents were "old timers" (experienced Solid users), meaning the survey lacked the fresh perspective from newcomers that the group desired.
- **Erich Bremer** noted that 18 responses is not a large cohort, limiting the statistical significance.
- **Conclusion:** While the data is useful, a **"Version 2.0"** of the survey is needed to get broader and more insightful feedback.

### 3.2 Key Themes & Feedback from the Survey

#### 3.2.1 Tooling & Documentation
- The primary barrier to entry is the complexity of getting started, especially regarding the setup and configuration of the **Community Solid Server (CSS)**.
- **Erich Bremer** shared that one of his senior developers approached Solid about a year ago and found it difficult to get into, despite being quite capable.

#### 3.2.2 JavaScript Dominance
- A common complaint was the heavy focus on **JavaScript/TypeScript tooling**, which alienates developers from other language ecosystems (e.g., Python, PHP, Go).
- **Elf Pavlik** noted this as one of his own complaints and linked it to the potential of WebAssembly.

#### 3.2.3 Authentication & Authorization
- This was identified as **both the most difficult challenge and the most exciting feature** for new developers.
- **Roberto** observed that at a recent hackathon, authentication/authorization was the source of the most support requests. However, it was also the thing that got people most excited—seeing that they could own their data and permission it in nuanced ways.
- **Elf Pavlik's Advice:** He often advises newcomers to start developing **without authentication** to avoid early discouragement. Build something working with public read/write permissions first, then dive into authentication once you have a working prototype.

#### 3.2.4 Suggested Areas for Focus (ODI Recommendations)
- The survey suggested focusing on:
  - More approachable identity providers (like Keycloak).
  - Improving the CSS backend experience.

### 3.3 Discussion on Specific Points

#### 3.3.1 Solid OIDC vs. LWS Authentication
- **Elf Pavlik** (co-editor of Solid OIDC) shared his perspective that the **OpenID authentication suite from Linked Web Storage (LWS)** should supersede Solid OIDC.
- He sees no need to maintain two separate specifications and would prefer to focus resources on LWS, positioning it as the "next iteration" of Solid OIDC.

#### 3.3.2 Community Solid Server (CSS)
- **CSS** is recognized as the primary open-source Solid server for experimentation.
- **Challenges:** The server's reliance on **Component JS** and **dependency injection** makes it daunting for newcomers to set up and customize.
- **Alternative:** A PHP Solid server exists but has limited community involvement and appears less widely used.

#### 3.3.3 Authorization & Access Control
- **Dedicated Authorization Service:** **Elf Pavlik** proposed an architectural direction where authorization logic (WAC/ACP) is completely decoupled from applications and managed by a dedicated, specialized app or service. This approach was inspired by work presented by **Krzysztof** (authorization app with a reference implementation and paper).
- **Benefits for App Developers:** This would simplify development for most app creators, who would only need to understand basic access modes (like "read" or "write"), not the complexities of WAC or ACP. It isolates the responsibility for complex policy enforcement to a single component, allowing regular applications to focus on their core functionality.

#### 3.3.4 WebAssembly (Wasm) as a Solution to Language Fragmentation
- **Elf Pavlik** highlighted that WebAssembly (especially with the component model) could be a game-changer for Solid tooling.
- **Theoretical Benefit:** Developers could write tools in their preferred language (Python, PHP, Java, Go, etc.), compile them to Wasm, and reuse them across different host languages.
- **Current Status:** Python can already host Wasm components (though not fully supported yet), and this capability is growing for other languages.
- **Conclusion:** Good Wasm tooling would significantly broaden the pool of developers who can contribute to and use Solid.

## 4. Announcements & Community Updates

### 4.1 EDMA (Enterprise Data Management Association) Community of Practice (Presented by Roberto)

#### 4.1.1 Background and Context
- This is a joint initiative by the **ODI (Open Data Institute)** and the **EDMA** to introduce Solid to a new audience of enterprise data professionals, primarily from the **finance and healthcare** sectors.
- **EDMA Overview:** The EDMA is an organization (described as a "guild") that brings together chief data officers and their teams from a large membership base across various sectors. Most members are in the United States, Western Europe, and the UK.
- **Initial Contact:** The ODI was introduced to EDMA through **Oli Beige** (formerly of the London Stock Exchange), who had previously championed the **CDMC (Cloud Data Management Protocol)** through EDMA. Oli described EDMA as one of the most valuable forums for getting feedback on standards and driving adoption.

#### 4.1.2 Recent Progress
- **Webinar Success:** A recent introductory webinar for EDMA members was a massive success—it was the **biggest webinar EDMA has ever had**.
- **Community of Practice Launch:** Weekly meetings are planned to start from **mid-September**.
- **Open to All:** The community of practice is public and anyone can join.

#### 4.1.3 Goals and Structure
- **Phase 1 (Initial Months):** Focus on educating new entrants—explaining what Solid is, how it works, and where it might be useful.
- **Phase 2 (Knowledge Exchange):** Once participants are up to speed, the conversation will shift to gathering feedback on barriers to adoption, missing features, and requirements to feed back into the Solid CG, LWS, and server implementers.
- **Matchmaking:** The group also aims to facilitate collaborations (e.g., connecting pod providers with interested organizations).
- **Outputs:** All outputs will be public and fed directly into the CG and relevant working groups.

#### 4.1.4 W3C Bureaucratic Angle
- The EDMA microsite mentions W3C links (specifically LWS) to help justify participation for employees of large organizations that have internal programs for W3C-related work.
- **Roberto clarified:** This is a practical measure to facilitate involvement, not a claim that Solid itself is a W3C standard. It conflates Solid and LWS for simplicity to avoid confusing the target audience.
- **Elf and Roberto agreed:** Work is needed to develop a clear, shared language to communicate the relationship between Solid and LWS. The current text oversimplifies the distinction.

#### 4.1.5 Roles and Ecosystem Thinking
- The EDMA initiative introduces the concept of **different roles** within the Solid ecosystem (e.g., pod provider, app developer, data consumer). This helps organizations understand where they fit and how they can contribute without feeling they need to "boil the ocean."
- The goal is to push people into these roles so they can work together to build a functioning ecosystem.

### 4.2 Paul Worrall's Carbon Accounting Project
- **Paul Worrall** shared that he previously built a federated carbon emissions reporting prototype for the EDMA for a **UN Climate Change event in Egypt (2023)**.
- **Current Relevance:** This use case is still relevant to his work, and he will present on it in a few weeks as part of his upcoming meeting with the group.
- **Related Efforts:** **Roberto** noted that **David Ottenhamer** (Interrupt) had done some related work, and **Jeff Zucker** may have connections to a Swiss group working on similar topics.

### 4.3 Erich Bremer's LWS Implementations

#### 4.3.1 Keycloak Integration (LWS-authn)
- **Erich Bremer** announced an open-source implementation of LWS authentication as custom providers for **Keycloak** (project name: `lws-authn`).
- **Status:** The source code is open and publicly available, but it is a preliminary version. Erich is currently testing it against the LWS specifications.
- **Next Step:** Once the Keycloak providers prove they work per the spec, Erich plans to explore how this integrates with Solid.

#### 4.3.2 Java LWS Reference Server
- **Erich** also made public a minimal **Java-based LWS reference server** built on Spring Boot (designed as a servlet).
- **Features:** It is fairly minimal and can compile to a single executable file using **GraalVM** for easy deployment.
- **Additional Features:** Erich added some non-spec features (e.g., HTTP digest headers) to demonstrate what an extended server might look like and to provide useful functionality. These are presented via the storage description.
- **Halcyon:** Erich mentioned another, more complex LWS server he is working on called "Halcyon," which is receiving more of his attention.

## 5. Future Topic: Business Logic & Data Integrity Enforcement

### 5.1 The Challenge
- **Elf Pavlik** introduced a new, non-urgent topic for future exploration: how to enforce complex, cross-resource business rules on a Solid Pod.
- **Example Use Case:** Preventing double-booking of a room across different booking resources. This requires validation that spans multiple resources and is more sophisticated than basic shape validation.

### 5.2 The Problem
- If users can choose their own applications, relying on a single app for business rule enforcement is insufficient. The data itself needs a system-level integrity mechanism.
- **Current State:** Solid can enforce basic shape conformance (e.g., requiring resources in a container to conform to a specific shape) and do server-side validation, but more complex business logic is not well-addressed.

### 5.3 Proposed Direction
- **Elf** suggested looking into how **LWS uses storage descriptions for service discovery** as a possible approach to solving this challenge.
- The idea is to explore how to enforce application-agnostic business logic at the storage level, giving resource owners control over data integrity regardless of which application their users choose.
- **Next Steps:** This will be a future discussion topic, not urgent but important for the long-term robustness of the Solid ecosystem.
